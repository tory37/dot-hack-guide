A spoiler-light field guide

.hack//consumed What to watch, what to play, and what you can safely skip.

.hack wasn't a game that got an anime, or an anime that got games. It was built from day one as one story told across several media, with each piece covering a different slice of a single timeline. That makes the entry point confusing. It also makes the answer to "what order?" pretty clear.

Is it all interconnected?
Yes.

Anime, games, OVAs, novels and manga all sit on one chronological timeline. Their contents don't overlap.

Can I just watch the anime?
Not really.

The series are prologues. The big payoffs happen in the games. There's a screen-only route, but it has gaps.

Do I need every game?
Not strictly.

Not for the whole story. If you only want one playable arc, G.U. Last Recode is a single modern package, but it skips the start and the end.

The Original Era

The first version of the VR game The World. The anime comes first, then a four-disc PS2 story with real-world OVA episodes alongside it.

  1. Anime

    .hack//SIGN

    26 episodesTV anime, 2002Start of Gen 1

    A slow-burn, atmospheric mystery about a player named Tsukasa who can't log out of the game. It sets up the world, the lore and the threat that hangs over everything after it.

    It's the prequel. The PS2 games begin right after it ends.

    How to watchStreaming rights change by region, so check your usual anime services. Physical copies exist too. The 2015 complete-series DVD set also includes the Unison bonus OVA.
  2. Game+ OVA

    The IMOQ quadrilogy + Liminality

    4 games, PS2one continuous story4 OVA episodes alongside

    .hack//Infection, Mutation, Outbreak, Quarantine. You play as Kite, who is trying to save players stuck in comas and stop a rogue AI. The four discs form one linear story, so play them in order.

    .hack//Liminality is a 4-episode OVA that shows the real world while Kite plays. It was originally bundled with the games, one episode per disc. Watch each episode after you finish the matching game.

    Access problemThese games only ran on the PS2 and feel dated. If you can't get a PS2 (or run your own discs through an emulator), a story recap or let's play on YouTube is a fair substitute. Used discs are often around $10–20 each and full sets around $100–130, but prices move.
  3. NovelsOptional

    .hack//Another Birth

    4 volumesEnglish: Tokyopop, 2006–2007

    A novel retelling of the four PS2 games. It's a good, cheap alternative if you can't play the discs, and a rewarding re-read if you did.

  4. Manga / AnimeOptional

    .hack//Legend of the Twilight

    Anime: 12 episodes, 2003Manga: 3 volumes, 2002–2004

    Twins Shugo and Rena get avatars styled after the game's legendary heroes Kite and BlackRose. It takes place about four years after Quarantine.

    The manga is canon. The anime isn't quite. The two match for roughly the first four episodes and then split into different stories. Read the manga if you want the canon version. Watch the anime only as a completionist.

2

The Modern Era: G.U.

Years later, in The World R:2, which is a grittier and more chaotic update of the game. This is the easiest place to jump in, and the only arc you can play on current consoles.

  1. Anime

    .hack//Roots

    26 episodesTV anime, 2006Start of Gen 2

    A player named Haseo joins a guild and lives through a run of tragedies. Like SIGN, it's a pure prequel.

    It ends exactly where the game begins. G.U. Rebirth picks up directly from its final moments.

    ShortcutShort on time? The G.U. Trilogy film (see Extras) condenses the G.U. story into one movie that continues from Roots. It's a recap, not a replacement for playing.
  2. Game

    .hack//G.U. Last Recode

    PS4 · PC (Steam) · Switch4 volumesRemaster, 2017 (Switch: 2022)

    A fully restored remaster of the three PS2 G.U. games (Rebirth, Reminisce, Redemption) plus a brand-new fourth volume, Reconnection. Reconnection is a short epilogue set about a year and three months after the main story, built around a promise Haseo couldn't keep.

    This is the one part of the franchise you can play easily today, which is why the smallest path, C, is built on it.

  3. FilmOptional

    .hack//G.U. Trilogy

    CG filmaired Dec 2007, on disc Jan 2008

    A condensed CG retelling of the G.U. story that continues from Roots. It's the quick option if you only want the plot. You'll miss the gameplay and the Reconnection epilogue.

3

The "Beyond" Era

If you finish G.U. and want more, the lore continues. It gets fragmented from here, and several pieces never left Japan.

  1. Game

    .hack//Link

    PSPJapan only, March 2010set in The World R:X

    Takes place roughly two years after G.U. It follows a junior-high student, Tokio Kuryuu, who is pulled into the game and saved by Kite. The story is told in a manga-like style, and several characters return from earlier entries.

    Needs a patchIt was never officially localized. A community English translation patch covers the full story (the final v2.0 release came in December 2023). You'll need a PSP or an emulator.
  2. OVA

    .hack//Quantum

    3 episodesKinema Citrus, 2011

    A short OVA released in early 2011 and best treated as a compact side story. Its exact slot in the timeline is loosely defined, so there's no penalty for watching it on its own.

  3. Film

    .hack//The Movie Beyond the World

    CG filmJan 2012set in 2024

    Set a few years after Link, this is the latest chapter of the in-universe timeline. A new player, Sora Yuki, sees a girl attacked by a smoke-like entity in The World, and soon players start losing consciousness while logged in.

  4. GameOptional

    .hack//Versus

    PS3 fighting gameJapan only, bundled with The Movie

    A spin-off fighter released alongside the film. It never received an English release. This one is for completionists only.

05 · FAQ

The questions everyone asks first.

Is .hack really one continuous story?

Yes. The anime, games, OVAs, manga and novels tell distinct, non-overlapping parts of one timeline. That's unlike franchises where the anime just re-tells the game. The only real exception is the anime version of Legend of the Twilight, which leaves the canon manga after a few episodes.

Can I skip the games and just watch the anime and movies?

You can, but you'll get the setup without most of the payoff. SIGN and Roots are prologues, and their big climaxes live in the games. Path B shows the best screen-only route: the G.U. Trilogy film condenses the G.U. story, and Liminality plus a recap covers the PS2 games. I'm not aware of an anime that retells the PS2 story, and Liminality shows the real-world side only. Expect gaps there.

Do I need to play all the games?

For the whole story, yes: the four PS2 games are the foundation of Generation 1, and Link closes Generation 3 (it needs a fan patch). For less, G.U. Last Recode is one package on PS4, PC and Switch, and Generation 2 stands on its own (Path C), but it skips the beginning and the end.

Should I follow release order or chronological order?

Here they're nearly the same: Gen 1 (2002–03), then Gen 2 (2006–07), then Gen 3 (2010–12). The guide is laid out that way. The communities don't agree on one "official" order, so treat this as a solid default rather than a rule.

I've never seen any of it. Where do I start?

Start with SIGN and follow Path A. It's the intended beginning. If you can't commit to the whole thing, Path B covers it on screen, and Path C (Roots then G.U. Last Recode) is the smallest self-contained arc, for when you'd rather get straight to a game you can play today.

The PS2 games sound hard to get. Any way around it?

Used PS2 discs are often around $10–20 each, with complete sets going for roughly $100–130, so they're findable but the set adds up. If that's a no, the Another Birth novels, a YouTube story recap or a let's play will carry you through, then pick up at Roots.

06 · Fact-check

How the source material held up.

This guide started from an AI-written chat rundown. Each claim was checked against reference sites and news coverage. Here's what held, what needed fixing, and what couldn't be confirmed.

Confirmed

SIGN → games → Liminality. The four PS2 games begin right after SIGN ends. Liminality is four OVA episodes bundled one per game and set at the same time as them.

Confirmed

Roots → G.U. Roots is 26 episodes. G.U. Rebirth picks up directly from where it leaves off.

Confirmed

Last Recode. It's the three G.U. games remastered plus the new Volume 4, Reconnection. PS4 and PC came in 2017, and Switch followed in March 2022.

Confirmed

Gen 3. Link is a Japan-only PSP game (2010) with a complete community English patch. Quantum is a 3-episode OVA (2011). The Movie arrived January 2012.

Corrected

"Legend of the Twilight diverges halfway." The anime actually branches from the manga after about the first four of twelve episodes. The manga is the canon version.

Corrected

"PS2 copies are incredibly expensive." Individual discs commonly sell for around $10–20. Complete sets run roughly $100–130 (a few spot prices, so treat them as rough). It's the full set that's pricey, not each disc.

Added

Things the original left out: the G.U. Trilogy film, the Another Birth novels, the Unison OVA, Japan-only Versus, Fragment and Zero, and the new .hack//Z.E.R.O. announced in 2026.

Unverified

"Quantum is highly rated" and "a great standalone." I found the release facts but no solid source for the quality claim or its exact timeline slot, so this guide only calls it a side story.
